# The History of Berania — story canon

Story revision 1, November 1, 2026. The user's correction is the authority for these events and overrides the prototype's earlier generic exile story. This document improves wording and pacing without changing the requested premise. Character and house names remain open; Greyford, Captain Mira, and Oathkeeper are provisional demo names.

## The promise

You were born the son of an archduke, raised close to Berania's imperial family. The Emperor's daughter was your childhood companion. You learned together, played together, and grew up imagining a future in which you would never have to part.

The princess stood first in the line of succession. Your father and the Emperor watched the affection between you deepen and promised that, when the time came, you would marry. What began as a bond between two children became a shared future between two families—and, one day, a future for the empire.

## The night of the coup

The Emperor's second son, born to a concubine, assassinated his father and seized the throne. He imprisoned his sister, the rightful heir, in a dungeon. Her claim threatened the crown he had taken; you and your father became targets of the coup.

You fought beside your father through the castle. When the pursuit closed in, the archduke stayed behind so you could escape. His final command was simple:

> “Save the princess. Save the empire. Live long enough to keep that promise.”

He gave his life buying you the time to reach the mech bay. You climbed into one of the castle's walkers and fought your way through the gates. You escaped alone. No household soldiers reached the road with you. No inheritance or treasury remained within your reach. You had only a battered mech and the promise your father had left you.

## The fracture

Berania's twenty-two major nobles divided after the assassination. Ten separated from the empire and founded their own factions. The remaining twelve joined the new emperor as imperial loyalists. Their motives can differ, but their initial allegiance is established; they are not an undecided neutral bloc.

The new emperor is hunting you. You are a fugitive with no starting gold, army, or secure home. Restoring the princess cannot begin with a royal campaign. It begins with finding work, keeping the walker running, and deciding whom to trust.

The campaign's purpose is to free the princess, restore the true heir, and save the empire. The player's cause supports her claim; the marriage promise does not make the protagonist the rightful emperor.

## From survival to restoration

The recommended dramatic progression keeps the existing five-chapter framework:

1. **The Exile:** Escape is told through short illustrated memories. At Greyford, defend a village to earn repairs, first pay, and local trust. Its defenders are temporary local allies. You still need to recruit an army of your own.
2. **The Unknown Noble:** Work, trade, and protect travelers while learning which rulers might hear your cause. Let people judge your actions before asking them to risk their lives for your name.
3. **A Banner Reborn:** Establish a modest headquarters, recruit a formal force, and make the first limited political pact. Breakaway rulers need reasons to support restoration: independence from the usurper does not imply loyalty to the princess.
4. **The Gathering Storm:** Turn that foothold into a coalition capable of resisting imperial forces. Earlier agreements, wars, and treatment of defeated nobles determine who stands with you.
5. **Return to Berania:** Reach the imprisoned princess, confront the usurper, and restore her claim. Rescue and restoration are related goals; freeing her does not instantly end the war or reunite every domain.

These are pacing recommendations, not five new prototype features. Keep the current village slice small.

## Character and presentation guidance

- Build the relationship through a few specific shared memories and later conversations. The princess should have a personality, judgment, and a voice in the restoration. Her future actions and precise role remain to be authored; do not invent an escape or resistance network as settled canon.
- The promised marriage anchors their history. Treat their present relationship as something they can express themselves, while preserving the requested goal of saving her and restoring her throne.
- The second son's responsibility for the murder is established for the player. There is no “unknown assassin” premise to solve. What different NPCs know or believe can vary without changing who committed the crime. His wrongdoing is his murder and seizure of power; his mother's status supplies family context, not a moral explanation.
- Keep pursuit readable: an imperial wanted notice, short reports, and later one authored pursuer encounter using existing party slots. No separate heat meter, constant chase timer, new survival resources, or extra simulated armies are required. The current demo communicates the hunt through narration, Mira's warning, and a wanted label; it does not yet simulate imperial pursuit. Greyford's roadwardens remain local defenders, not imperial hunters.
- Use **Emperor** for Berania's ruler, **usurper/new emperor** for the second son, and **princess/rightful heir** for his imprisoned sister. Do not silently turn the player into the heir to the imperial throne.
