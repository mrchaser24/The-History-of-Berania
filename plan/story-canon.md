# The History of Berania — story canon

**Story revision:** 2 — November 1, 2026

**Status:** User-established plot and choices, with clearly labeled authored draft additions. These events supersede revision 1 and incompatible older roadmap text. They describe the intended campaign, not newly implemented gameplay.

Oathkeeper is the approved starting mech. Names and ages of people, houses, and supporting characters below are draft writing choices, not separate user approvals of their art or final dialogue. The current demo remains a small narrated opening.

## World and tone

Berania is a medieval magitech empire: hereditary rulers, swords, shields, cavalry, early firearms, magic, and rare military mechs coexist. Firearms are scarce, expensive, and early enough that traditional arms remain useful. Great houses control the specialists and workshops needed to maintain mechs.

The tone is a dark political tragedy with an earned happy ending possible. Love, duty, grief, and the temptation to rule drive the story. The protagonist can keep his father's promise or abandon it for his own crown. Understanding an antagonist's suffering does not excuse his later choices.

## The promise and the three friends

The protagonist, Aurelia, and Lucien grow up together. The two young men are close enough to think of one another as brothers. The protagonist and Aurelia genuinely love each other and freely welcome the marriage their fathers promise.

Before the persecution, Lucien accepts Aurelia's succession and intends neither to harm her nor steal her crown. His later actions destroy real family bonds and a real friendship.

## The crimes before the coup

Aldren secretly has Selene assassinated to erase the shame he associates with having a concubine. Lucien grieves and grows angry. Aldren's paranoia then convinces him that his second son threatens Aurelia's life or succession, despite Lucien's innocence.

Aldren prepares to kill Lucien and purge his circle. Roland is kept in the dark about the murder and planned persecution. The protagonist and Aurelia also begin without the full truth.

Lucien learns he is in danger and strikes first. His initial motive is survival, not an existing plot against his sister.

## The coup, sacrifice, and hunt

Lucien assassinates Aldren, takes the throne, and publicly claims that overthrowing him was necessary to stop a murderous purge. His responsibility for the assassination is established; there is no unknown-assassin mystery.

He imprisons Aurelia in a dungeon. He fears she will avenge their father or deliver him to hostile nobles. He still loves his sister and cannot bring himself to order her death. His fear becomes a need to control her and keep power; over time he also comes to want the authority itself.

Lucien wrongly believes Roland helped Aldren persecute him and that the protagonist was an accomplice. Draft connective detail: Garrick reinforces this accusation to win the confiscated duchy. The imperial hunt seeks the protagonist's capture, imprisonment, and eventual execution.

During the castle escape, Roland stays behind to hold the pursuit. Voss's force kills him. Roland's last request remains:

> “Save the princess. Save the empire. Live long enough to keep that promise.”

The protagonist fights his way to Oathkeeper and escapes alone. No household troops, starting gold, treasury, or usable estate accompany him. The machine is damaged. Greyford's later defenders belong to their settlement and are temporary allies.

## The fracture: 22 = 10 + 12

The opening political register contains 22 major noble seats. Ten secede into ten independent factions competing over who will rule Berania. They form temporary blocs and fight one another; none is automatically an ally of the princess.

Twelve initially support Lucien. Garrick occupies Roland's former seat among those twelve. The dead archduke is not an additional living ruler or a twenty-third seat. The imperial family is separate from this vassal register.

## Five-act campaign

### Act I — The broken promise

The current illustrated memories establish the promise, coup, sacrifice, and fracture. At Greyford, the protagonist earns repairs, pay, and trust through village defense. No playable castle escape or full imperial pursuit is added merely by adopting this outline.

### Act II — A banner without a country

Work, trade, protect travelers, recruit troops and companion pilots, and earn a headquarters. Found the warband and create its banner. Noble support must be earned through motives and negotiated terms.

A required investigation reveals Aldren's persecution through preserved orders and corroborating testimony before the rescue decision. It must not depend on an optional companion. Aurelia also learns the truth during the campaign; on the restoration route they can discuss the complete evidence after rescue. Her precise information delivery remains authored scene work.

### Act III — The price of a crown

As the protagonist's army wins ground and approaches the capital, Rothmar, Vaust, and Merrow offer to switch sides. They see Lucien as unstable and expect the protagonist's reign to depend on their money and troops.

Their condition is abandoning Aurelia's rescue and restoration. Their reward is four veteran formations, one advanced mech frame delivered unassigned to the shared garage, and a substantial fixed gold grant. The exact gold amount and unit statistics are later balance values.

The decision is explicit and permanent. Show the terms before commitment. A hidden morality score cannot substitute for this choice.

- **Reject:** The three houses conclude they have no future under Aurelia and attempt to destroy the protagonist. They field the four formations and the same advanced frame under their own pilot. Defeating that force opens the rescue operation.
- **Accept:** The houses join the protagonist's cause and deliver the assets once. He abandons the rescue and commits to his own imperial claim. Accepting and later freeing her cannot reopen the restoration route.

The restoration rescue is a raid on the capital's prison around the campaign midpoint. It does not capture the capital or depose Lucien.

### Act IV — Restoration or dominion

On the restoration route, Aurelia leads the coalition politically and can deploy as a mech pilot using an assigned frame. She has opinions, diplomatic agency, and disagreements about wartime methods. Their existing love is tested by the people they become.

On the dark route, Aurelia organizes an escape with another ally's help, rather than waiting for the protagonist to change his mind. She survives abroad knowing he chose the bargain over her. Introduce the physician and opportunities for her agency before this escape occurs.

In either route, resolve the civil war through alliances, submission, and conquest. Capturing the capital alone is insufficient: every surviving major house must ultimately recognize the winning ruler. Succession outcomes preserve a route to recognition if a ruler dies.

### Act V — The throne's last prisoner

Lucien refuses surrender and dies fighting in the final confrontation. His childhood friendship with the protagonist remains part of the tragedy. There is no final mercy/execution selection for him.

The imperial throne contains a magitech mechanism that amplifies the ruler's magic and perception. Prolonged use feeds dependence, fear, and possessiveness. Lucien knowingly embraces the advantage; the mechanism amplifies his choices rather than removing responsibility. Whether it contributed to Aldren's paranoia remains uncertain.

- **Restoration ending:** Aurelia becomes empress, marries the protagonist, and rules with him as prince-consort and military partner. They destroy the magical mechanism while retaining the imperial office.
- **Dominion ending:** The protagonist establishes his own dynasty and takes the throne; he does not become the rightful heir by marriage or birth. Draft ending detail: he retains its mechanism and repeats the danger. Aurelia remains in exile.

These are the two major endings. Companion epilogues, individual noble settlements, and surviving relationships may vary within them without introducing another main route.

## Companion and noble roles

Allied and captured nobles are campaign-support characters, replacing the earlier proposal for 1–3 allied noble pilots in the player's personal battle roster. They supply troops, funding, access, territory, diplomacy, and allied armies. Capturing a noble does not automatically recruit them.

The protagonist, Aurelia, and up to five companions are the personal hero pilots. Every companion fights in a mech; experienced veterans without a machine need an assigned available frame before deploying.
